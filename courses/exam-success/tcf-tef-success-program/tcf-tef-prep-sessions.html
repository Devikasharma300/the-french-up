About This Course

You already have French. Now you need to learn how to use it efficiently under exam conditions.

TCF & TEF Canada Preparation is the final, exam-focused stage of our pathway. The emphasis shifts from building general French to handling the actual demands of the test: understanding the task quickly, organizing a response, managing time and communicating clearly within the time limit.

Along the way, you will learn to recognize the mistakes and habits that cost you time or weaken an otherwise good answer.

What We'll Practice

Practice is built around what you will actually need to do on exam day.

Speaking under time limits

Build answers quickly, organize them as you speak and keep going when a question catches you off guard.

Writing to the task

Plan quickly, organize ideas clearly and leave time to check your work.

Listening with a purpose

Identify the information that matters, follow changes in meaning and avoid getting stuck on one word you did not understand.

Reading efficiently

Locate key information, distinguish close answer choices and manage harder questions without losing too much time.

A typical Preparation cycle

1

Try the task

Work with an exam-style prompt or a timed mock section, first with support and then under tighter conditions.

2

Review the performance

Look at language, organization, timing, task completion and communication.

3

Do it again better

Rework the task or apply the same correction to a new prompt.

TCF Canada Preparation

Prepare for the three-task speaking and writing formats, while building speed for the listening and reading sections.

Listening & Reading

  • Listening: 39 multiple-choice questions · 35 minutes
  • Reading: 39 multiple-choice questions · 60 minutes

Speaking & Writing

  • Speaking: 3 tasks · 12 minutes
  • Writing: 3 tasks · 60 minutes

In TCF practice, we'll work on

  • asking relevant questions naturally in the interaction task
  • developing and defending a point of view with enough detail
  • respecting the purpose, audience and word requirements of each written task

Official TCF Canada information: France Éducation international .

TEF Canada Preparation

Prepare for the TEF's two-part speaking and writing tasks, with focused work on interaction, persuasion and timed production.

Listening & Reading

  • Listening: 40 questions · 40 minutes
  • Reading: 40 questions · 60 minutes

Speaking & Writing

  • Speaking: 2 sections · 15 minutes
  • Writing: 2 sections · 60 minutes

In TEF practice, we'll work on

  • asking enough useful questions in the information-gathering task
  • building convincing arguments and responding when the examiner challenges your position
  • continuing an article coherently in Writing Section A
  • developing and justifying a clear point of view in Writing Section B

Official TEF Canada information: Le français des affaires .

Who Should Attend

Preparation may be right for you if...

  • you are around B1+ or above and can already manage longer conversations and written responses
  • you know which exam you are taking: TCF Canada or TEF Canada
  • you need practice with format, timing, strategy and performance under exam conditions
  • you want detailed correction on speaking and writing
  • you are ready to practise between sessions and apply feedback to new tasks

You may need a different starting point if...

  • you are completely new to French or still building basic sentences
  • you still struggle to build longer answers or communicate consistently
  • you mainly want general conversation lessons rather than exam-specific work

The French Up Approach

Practice the exam without reducing French to a formula.

We make the format familiar.

You practise the task types, timing and interaction you will face so exam day feels less unfamiliar.

We give feedback you can use.

Feedback addresses grammar, vocabulary, clarity, organization and communication, with clear priorities for your next attempt.

We add difficulty as your control improves.

Longer answers, time limits and exam-style constraints are added as your control improves, so you can build accuracy before working under more pressure.

We build skills that carry over.

The goal isn't to perform one rehearsed answer well. It's to become more capable of handling unfamiliar questions and tasks in French.
