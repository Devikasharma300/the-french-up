The diplomas explained

What are DELF and DALF?

DELF (Diplôme d’études en langue française) and DALF (Diplôme approfondi de langue française) are official French-language diplomas awarded by France’s Ministry of National Education. Unlike time-limited proficiency tests, these diplomas are valid for life.

DELF · A1–B2 Beginner to upper-intermediate French
DALF · C1–C2 Advanced to proficient French
Four language skills Listening, reading, speaking and writing

Wondering what A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 or C2 means? Read the French government’s guide to CEFR language levels ↗ for a clear description of each level.

For official exam information, available versions (including Junior and Tout Public), Canadian exam centres, registration and fees, visit Institut français du Canada — DELF/DALF exams ↗. The French Up offers preparation, not the official diploma examinations.

Understand the exams

DELF and DALF: what changes?

DELF · A1 to B2

Four assessed skills: listening, reading, writing and speaking. Tasks become more independent and argumentative as the level rises.

  • A1–A2: familiar situations, short messages and everyday interactions
  • B1: experiences, reasons, opinions and connected texts
  • B2: nuanced opinions, structured arguments and formal writing

DALF · C1 and C2

Advanced academic and professional communication, where clarity, precision and synthesis matter.

  • C1: synthesize documents, write an argument and give a structured oral presentation
  • C2: complete integrated listening–speaking and reading–writing tasks

Exam formats and sample papers: France Éducation international. The diploma exam is booked separately.
