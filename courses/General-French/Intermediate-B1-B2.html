Build independent communication

About This Course

Move beyond basic French and develop the confidence to communicate independently in professional and social situations.

This structured intermediate course strengthens your language skills through conversation, grammar practice, listening, reading, and writing. You will learn to share experiences, explain ideas, and participate in increasingly detailed discussions.

Your learning journey

Start with practical B1 tasks—handling everyday situations, describing experiences, and explaining simple opinions—then develop toward B2 tasks, including fuller discussions, more complex texts and confident professional communication.

Important note on learning pace

Everyone learns at a different pace. Progress may vary depending on your previous experience, current language level, learning style, and the amount of practice you are able to do outside of class.

Some learners may need additional time, review, or practice to fully develop the skills covered in this course.

The learning outcomes describe the skills you will work toward during the course, but reaching B1 or B2 may require additional practice and continued use of French in real-life situations.

Course curriculum

What You'll Learn

Strengthen your speaking, grammar, and communication skills for a wider range of situations.

Speaking

  • Tell stories and describe experiences
  • Explain plans and give reasons
  • Express and support opinions with examples
  • Take part in longer discussions
  • Build fluency and responsiveness

Grammar

  • Past and future tenses
  • Conditional structures
  • Introduction to the subjunctive
  • Longer, more complex sentences
  • Linking ideas in connected arguments

Communication

  • Everyday exchanges, travel, shopping and services
  • Workplace conversations
  • Current events and cultural discussions
  • Understanding spoken French and written texts
  • Writing connected messages and short texts

These topics are illustrative examples, not an exhaustive list.

Speaking, grammar and communication are integrated with listening, reading, writing and pronunciation throughout the course. Lesson difficulty is adapted to the group's starting level.

Recommended starting point

Prerequisites

Prior French study is required.

This is an Elementary to Developing Intermediate program, generally suited to learners around CEFR A2 and moving toward B1–B2.

You should consider this course if:

  • you’ve completed French Essentials (A1–A2), or have equivalent basic French
  • you can hold simple conversations but are not yet ready for intensive exam preparation
  • you understand many basic grammar concepts but still struggle to use them consistently in real communication
  • you can understand some spoken and written French but lose accuracy when the language becomes faster or more complex
You do not need to identify your own CEFR level perfectly. If you are unsure whether you belong in the French Intermediate (B1–B2) program, contact us and we can help confirm the best starting point.

Understanding French levels

What do B1 and B2 actually mean?

B1 and B2 are the two intermediate levels of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), a widely used guide to language ability. In simple terms, B1 means you can manage familiar situations on your own, while B2 means you can handle more detailed conversations, ideas and texts with greater ease.

B1 · Independent communicator

At this level, learners are able to:

  • Understand the main ideas in clear conversations and texts about familiar subjects
  • Manage common situations while travelling
  • Write connected paragraphs about familiar topics
  • Describe experiences, plans and goals, and briefly explain your opinions

B2 · Confident communicator

At this level, learners are able to:

  • Interact with fluent French speakers with less strain for both sides
  • Express, explain and support opinions clearly
  • Understand more complex texts and discussions
  • Take an active part in many professional or academic conversations

Why B2 matters: Many learners see it as an important milestone on the path toward fluency. This course helps you work toward B1–B2 abilities, but reaching a particular level depends on your starting point and practice outside class.
